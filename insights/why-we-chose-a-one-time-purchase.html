Why We Chose a One-Time Purchase

Subscriptions can be the right model for services with continuous infrastructure and operating costs. But not every piece of software needs to become a recurring bill.

Memmola LabsJuly 29, 2026About 7 minutes

Subscriptions are not automatically wrong

A recurring subscription can be reasonable when a product depends on ongoing cloud computing, hosted storage, large online databases, live collaboration, human services or frequently changing licensed content.

Those systems create continuing costs each time a customer uses them. A recurring payment can help keep the service available, secure and maintained.

The problem begins when subscription pricing becomes the default even for products whose essential function runs locally and does not create a meaningful per-use server cost.

The question we asked

Does SlangBit need a permanent payment relationship in order to perform its core function?

SlangBit is primarily local software

SlangBit runs as a Windows utility. After the application and selected language packages are installed, translation inference takes place on the user’s own computer.

The core workflow does not depend on sending every translation to infrastructure operated by Memmola Labs. There is no metered cloud translation request behind each press of ALT + Z.

That architecture influenced the commercial model. If the main processing is performed by the customer’s computer, requiring an indefinite subscription for access to the basic translation workflow would be difficult to justify.

A utility should feel like a tool you bought

Traditional desktop utilities were often purchased because they solved a specific problem. You installed the tool, learned its workflow and continued using it.

A one-time purchase preserves more of that relationship. The customer makes a clear decision once instead of adding another small monthly charge to an expanding collection of subscriptions.

This is especially relevant for software intended to stay quietly in the Windows tray. SlangBit is designed to disappear when it is not needed, not to demand constant attention or repeatedly justify a monthly renewal.

The hidden cost of recurring software

A low monthly price may appear easier than a single purchase, but recurring software creates a different kind of cost: another renewal to remember, another charge to monitor and another product whose usefulness must be reassessed every month or year.

For a frequently used professional service, that trade-off may be acceptable. For a focused desktop utility, it can become unnecessary friction.

Recurring access

  • Lower initial commitment
  • Continuous billing relationship
  • Access may stop when payments stop
  • Best suited to continuing services

One-time purchase

  • Clear upfront decision
  • No recurring charge for core access
  • Better fit for a local utility
  • Simple long-term ownership model

One-time purchase does not mean zero ongoing cost

Offline software still needs maintenance. Development, testing, Windows compatibility, Store certification, support, documentation, package hosting, security work and future updates all require time and resources.

A one-time purchase therefore has to be priced carefully. It must remain accessible while still contributing to the long-term sustainability of the product.

The decision is not based on the idea that software should be free forever after release. It is based on matching the payment model to the technical and operational structure of the product.

FITThe business model should reflect how the software actually works.
CLARITYThe customer should understand what is purchased without decoding a pricing ladder.
SUSTAINABILITYThe product still needs enough revenue to remain maintained and supported.

Why we did not hide the product behind usage limits

Another possible model would have been to charge for translation volume: a certain number of characters, requests or language pairs each month.

That model makes sense when every request consumes paid server infrastructure. It makes less sense when the translation is generated locally using resources already available on the customer’s computer.

Artificial usage limits would add complexity to a product whose purpose is to reduce friction.

What a one-time purchase does and does not promise

A one-time purchase should be clear, but it should not create unrealistic expectations.

It provides access to the product offered under that purchase and the supported maintenance associated with it. It does not necessarily guarantee every future major edition, every new service or every product Memmola Labs may create.

Major changes in technology, operating systems or product scope may eventually require a separate version or commercial decision. If that happens, it should be explained openly rather than hidden behind ambiguous wording.

Our principle

No recurring subscription is required to keep using the core SlangBit product purchased through the Microsoft Store.

Why this choice matters for trust

Pricing communicates how a company expects to relate to its users. A subscription says that access is an ongoing service. A one-time purchase says that the customer is buying a tool.

For SlangBit, the second description is more accurate. It is a focused Windows utility built around local models, a global shortcut and a private workflow.

The payment model is therefore not only a marketing decision. It is part of the product philosophy: local processing, direct control and fewer recurring dependencies.

Frequently asked questions

Why does SlangBit use a one-time purchase?

Because its core translation workflow runs locally on the customer’s Windows computer rather than relying on a permanent cloud translation service.

Does a one-time purchase mean there are no maintenance costs?

No. Development, support, testing, distribution and package hosting continue to create costs. The one-time model reflects the absence of a recurring per-translation cloud service, not the absence of ongoing work.

Will the purchased version stop working if I do not pay again?

The core product purchased through the Microsoft Store does not require a recurring subscription to remain accessible.

Does the purchase include every future Memmola Labs product?

No. The purchase applies to SlangBit under the terms offered for that product. Separate products or major future editions may have their own terms.

Why not offer a free product supported by advertising?

Advertising would introduce another relationship involving attention, tracking or external services. That would not fit naturally with a focused, privacy-oriented desktop utility.
